## 6. Sales techniques / patterns

1. **Pad every line slightly upward** ($345 → $365 fan kit, $509.25 → $525 plenum kit, $2,250 → $2,270 install) — Drew: "make it look like we did a little bit of calculations". Two purposes: (a) extra margin for absorbing the discount; (b) makes the number look researched, not rounded.
2. **Quote mixed vent lengths** (3× 4ft + 1× 3ft + 2× 1ft instead of clean 4× 4ft) so installer has flexibility on site. "Method to this little madness."
3. **Worst-case scenario on dimensions** when customer is undecided: "whenever we're back and forth, we always go with the worst case scenario — always cool with the high end."
4. **Discount conversion to dollar language for residential customers.** Never percent.
5. **Explicit "placeholder price subject to change"** in line item description for custom limestone — manages expectations + protects margin on the actual measure.
6. **Generic note line** "Builder to do framing, gas, and electric" — clarifies what is NOT in the quote.
7. **"R76C silicone goes on EVERY order with venting or chimney"** — universal SKU rule the trainee must memorize.
8. **Naming custom items with `BS-FP-` prefix** (Drew corrected the trainee: "BSFP" → "BS-FP-" with the dash) when creating new items in BisTrack.
9. **Customer-read-through:** Drew notices Carol's "we might go with these guys" was a tactical bluff; he sees through it but plays along graciously: "we are lucky enough to have a good reputation."
10. **Save preliminary quote as PDF** with naming convention: `[CustomerLastName] Preliminary Estimate` → email to customer + CC the trainee since the trainee will own the job once commissioned.
